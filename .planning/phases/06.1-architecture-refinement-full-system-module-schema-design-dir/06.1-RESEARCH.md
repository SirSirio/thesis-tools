## Summary

This phase turns `tools/system-architecture-explorer/` from a pump-control matrix into a whole-device
page. The two hardest parts — the six research questions in CONTEXT.md D-15/D-16 and the D-18
consistency pass — are both fully resolvable with sourced data, gathered below.

**Headline findings:**
- **28BYJ-48: use the 12 V variant.** The manufacturer datasheet shows the common 12 V/200 Ω winding
  draws *less* current (~60 mA/phase) *and* delivers *more* pull-in torque (≥34.3 mN·m) than the 5 V/60 Ω
  winding (~83 mA/phase, ≥29.4 mN·m). bitbyg sells both at the identical price (25 DKK, selectable
  dropdown) — 12 V is strictly better with zero cost penalty.
- **Power rails: two small buck converters off the existing 24 V rail**, not a raw "12 V+24 V dual
  supply." A 24 V→12 V adjustable buck (LM2596-class, bitbyg 22.50–23.75 DKK) feeds the two 28BYJ-48s;
  a 24 V→5 V fixed synchronous buck (bitbyg 42.50 DKK, 5 A) feeds ESP32 + screen + SD + LM75 +
  capacitive module. Combined new draw on the 24 V rail is ≈0.39 A (≈9.4 W) — negligible against the
  existing 60–150 W PSU budget.
- **Vibration motor: PWM from the 5 V rail through a MOSFET + flyback diode, not 12 V direct-drive.**
  The physics prior in CONTEXT.md is confirmed: 12 V on a 3 V-rated brushed motor is a 16× power
  overdrive (P ∝ V²). Recommend an IRF520 driver module (bitbyg, 13.75 DKK) — flagged because it is
  **not** a logic-level part and needs verification against the ESP32's 3.3 V gate drive.
- **Capacitive touch controller is an MPR121** (NOT a TTP22x-family chip — the CONTEXT-SEED's guess was
  wrong), I²C, 12 usable electrodes (+1 virtual), bitbyg 47.50 DKK. One module covers all 6 storage
  containers with 6 electrodes to spare. It rides the *same* dedicated I²C attachment already paid for
  by the LM75 — **zero additional pin cost** in the existing `pinsOf()` model. Feasibility as a true
  liquid-level sensor (not just touch) is a genuine open risk — flagged below, not glossed over.
- **Temperature note:** water's viscosity changes ~100× faster with temperature than its volumetric
  thermal expansion coefficient does (≈2–3 %/°C vs ≈0.02–0.03 %/°C near room temperature) — the
  one-line UI note should foreground viscosity, mentioning volume-expansion only as the secondary,
  much smaller effect Sirio is more worried about.
- **Ground truth:** the `VARIANTS` array has **exactly 20** entries (not 19) — SPEC.md's Purpose
  paragraph and its "Variant BOMs" heading are the two places still wrong; its cross-links section
  already correctly says 20. A second, previously unflagged inconsistency was also found: SPEC.md
  claims "13 of 21 components," but the actual `DEFAULTS` object has **22** keys, of which **12** are
  High confidence — both numbers in that sentence are off by one.

**Primary recommendation:** Resolve D-15/D-16 with the 12 V 28BYJ-48 + dual-buck-converter topology,
adopt the MPR121-based capacitive model and MOSFET+PWM vibration-motor drive, and fix the exact stale
strings listed in Ground-Truth Findings below (file + line references included so the planner can
cite them verbatim).

## Hardware Research Findings (Q1–Q5)

### Q1 — 28BYJ-48: 5 V vs 12 V variant

**Recommendation: use the 12 V variant.** It is objectively better on both torque and current, at
zero cost difference.

**Datasheet numbers** (manufacturer datasheet, "Stepper motor 28BYJ-48 12V," electrical parameters
table cross-referencing both windings) — **HIGH confidence**, sourced PDF, standard part datasheet
found via multiple independent listings for this ubiquitous hobby part:

| Variant | Coil resistance | Pull-in torque | Current @ rated V (Ohm's law, single phase) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 V | 60 Ω/phase | ≥29.4 mN·m | 5 V / 60 Ω ≈ **83 mA/phase** |
| 12 V (200 Ω winding — the common bitbyg/hobby SKU) | 200 Ω/phase | ≥34.3 mN·m | 12 V / 200 Ω ≈ **60 mA/phase** |

Both share: 4-phase, 1/64 nominal gear ratio (⚠ actual internal ratio is **63.68395:1**, not exactly
64:1 — teeth-count analysis (31·32·26·22)/(11·10·9·9) = 63.68395; using the rounded 64:1 causes
~0.5 % positional drift over many revolutions — MEDIUM confidence, cross-referenced community
teardown analysis, non-blocking for this phase), 5.625°/64 step angle, ≥500 PPS pull-in / ≥900 PPS
pull-out frequency, ≤40 K temperature rise, ≤35 dB noise.

**Conclusion:** the 12 V/200 Ω winding draws **~28 % less current per phase** while delivering
**~17 % more torque** than the 5 V/60 Ω winding — there is no trade-off, only upside, *provided* a
12 V rail is available (which Q2 provides anyway for the "downsize from 24 V" requirement).

**Vendor stock — bitbyg** (HIGH confidence, live page fetch): the existing tool's `align28byj`
component already links the exact bitbyg listing —
[bitbyg "Step Gear Motor 28BYJ-48-5V 4 Fase 5 Ledning DC 5V 12V"](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/step-gear-motor-28byj-48-5v-4-fase-5-ledning-dc-5v-12v/)
— **both 5 V and 12 V are sold as a selectable dropdown option on the same listing, at the identical
price of 25 DKK.** No ULN2003 driver board is bundled (it is a separate line item, already the
`align28byj` DEFAULTS price includes it — the tool's existing 43.75 DKK = 25 DKK motor + 18.75 DKK
"Stepmotor Driver Board ULN2003" is correct and unaffected by the voltage choice).

**Action:** no BOM price change — `align28byj`'s existing €5.86 price is voltage-agnostic at bitbyg.
Only the *voltage rail feeding it* changes (see Q2). Update the component label from "28BYJ-48 12V +
ULN2003" (already correct!) — no label change needed, just confirm the 12 V choice is now a
research-backed decision, not an inherited assumption.

### Q2 — Step-down rail design from 24 V

**Recommendation: two small buck-converter modules off the existing 24 V rail — not a single "12 V
+24 V dual supply."** ARCHITECTURE.md's current phrasing ("likely a 12 V + 24 V dual rail") implies
two independently-generated supply voltages from the wall; in practice the PSU is a single 24 V
output (already modelled as `psu60`/`psu150`), and everything below 24 V is generated locally with
step-down converters — this is both cheaper and matches Sirio's own framing ("I will need to downsize
from 24 V anyways").

**Candidates evaluated** (bitbyg-sourced, HIGH confidence — live page fetches 2026-07-16):

| Option | Input | Output | Max current | Price (bitbyg) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [LM2596/LM2596S adjustable buck](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/dc-dc-step-down-lm2596-lm2596s/) | 4–35 V | 1.23–30 V adjustable | 3 A (heatsink recommended >2.5 A) | 22.50–23.75 DKK | **Recommended for the 12 V alignment rail** — set trim-pot to 12 V, load is ~0.25 A, no heatsink needed |
| [DC-DC Buck Step Down Synchronous Rectification](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/dc-dc-buck-step-down-synchronous-rectification-power-module/) | 9–35 V | 5 V fixed | 5 A (25 W) | 42.50 DKK | **Recommended for the 5 V logic rail** — fixed output avoids trim-pot drift, ample headroom |
| 7805 linear regulator | any | 5 V fixed | ~1 A (thermally limited) | not evaluated in detail — see below | **Rejected**: at 24 V→5 V with even 0.5 A load, the regulator must dissipate (24−5)×0.5 = **9.5 W** as heat with no heatsink margin on a small TO-220 part in an enclosed device — thermally infeasible without a large heatsink, and wastes ~80% of input power as heat vs a buck converter's ~85–92% efficiency. Confirmed by standard linear-regulator power-dissipation arithmetic (P = ΔV × I), not a vendor claim. |
| Multi-output bench PSU (e.g. the existing 12V-24V 200W-600W bitbyg unit already in `psu150`) | mains | fixed rails | high | already in BOM | Not applicable here — that unit outputs the 24 V *supply* rail itself, not a secondary step-down; keeping it separate from the two buck converters below is cleaner and matches the existing `psu60`/`psu150` role in the model |

**Recommended rail topology (with current-budget arithmetic):**

```
Mains → PSU (psu60/psu150, existing, unchanged) → 24 V rail
  ├─→ 6× DRV8825 → 6× NEMA17 pump steppers (existing, unchanged; ≈6.25 A @ 24V all-6-concurrent, psu150-sized)
  ├─→ [NEW] LM2596 buck (set to 12 V) → 2× 28BYJ-48 (via 2× ULN2003 boards)
  │     Load: 2 motors × ≤2 phases active × 60 mA/phase (12V/200Ω, Q1) ≈ 240 mA @ 12V
  │     P_out = 12V × 0.24A = 2.88 W → @ ~85% buck efficiency → P_in ≈ 3.39 W
  │     I_in from 24V rail ≈ 3.39W / 24V ≈ 0.14 A
  └─→ [NEW] Fixed 5V/5A synchronous buck → ESP32 + screen + SD + LM75 + MPR121 capacitive module
        Load budget: ESP32 ~500 mA (WiFi active, bursts to ~800 mA) + screen/backlight/touch ~150 mA
          + SD card ~100 mA (write peak) + LM75 ~1 mA (negligible) + MPR121 ~10-20 mA
          ≈ 780 mA nominal, ~1.1 A transient peak
        P_out = 5V × 1A ≈ 5 W → @ ~85% buck efficiency → P_in ≈ 5.88 W
        I_in from 24V rail ≈ 5.88W / 24V ≈ 0.25 A
```

**Total new draw added to the 24 V rail: ≈0.14 A + 0.25 A ≈ 0.39 A (≈9.4 W).** This is small relative
to the existing 60 W/150 W PSU sizing — **no change to PSU wattage selection is required.** This
figure is worth adding as an explicit new line in SPEC.md's power model, since the current model only
sizes the PSU for driver/motor concurrency and never previously accounted for logic/alignment/screen
current — MEDIUM confidence (the load estimates for ESP32/screen/SD current draw are typical
datasheet-class figures, not measured on the specific bitbyg parts; flag as an estimate pending
build-time verification).

**Vibration motor is NOT a separate regulated rail** — it draws from the 5 V rail through its PWM/MOSFET
drive circuit (see Q3); average current is small due to burst-only duty and is already covered by the
5 V rail's headroom above.

**MPR121 voltage caveat (flows into Q4):** the MPR121 module's datasheet-stated operating range is
**2.5–3.6 VDC** — it is **not** directly 5 V-tolerant. Recommend powering it from the ESP32 dev
board's onboard 3V3 pin (standard practice; typical ESP32 dev-board regulators such as AMS1117-3.3 are
rated 800 mA–1 A, and MPR121's current draw is a few mA) rather than adding a third regulated rail.
**LOW confidence** on the specific bitbyg ESP32 board's onboard regulator current rating — not stated
on its product page; flag as a build-time check, not a blocking issue (headroom is almost certainly
ample given MPR121's tiny current draw).

### Q3 — Vibration-motor drive scheme

**Physics check confirmed:** running a 3 V-rated brushed DC motor at 12 V direct drive is a genuine
16× overdrive. For a resistance-dominated brushed motor, dissipated power scales with V² (P = V²/R at
a fixed effective resistance); (12/3)² = 16. **This is not a guess — it follows directly from Ohm's
law/power law and is confirmed, not merely repeated from the CONTEXT.md prior.** Even short bursts at
4× rated voltage risk brush arcing, magnet demagnetization from excess current, and winding
insulation stress; there is no manufacturer overvoltage-burst rating available to make this a
qualified risk (see confidence note below), so it should be ruled out.

**Vendor part identified — NOT a coin/pager ERM vibration motor.** The linked bitbyg part
([bitbyg "3v Mikromotor til Gør-det-selv"](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/3v-micro-motor-for-diy/), 17.50 DKK,
HIGH confidence on price/voltage/dimensions — live page fetch) is model **RD520PA**, a conventional
small brushed permanent-magnet DC motor (21×38 mm, 2 mm shaft, 19 g) — the kind used in toy
cars/fans, not a purpose-built eccentric-mass vibration motor. A cross-reference search found the same
model attributed to a Chinese motor manufacturer with a torque rating of **300–350 gf·cm** — **MEDIUM
confidence** (secondary/manufacturer-listing source, not the bitbyg page itself, and no formal
datasheet was locatable). **No rated/stall current figure could be sourced for this specific motor —
this is a genuine gap, not a guess.** Estimating current draw for an RS-520-class small brushed motor
from typical family behavior (~100–250 mA nominal, stall current several ×) would be an unsourced
number and is explicitly **NOT** presented as a BOM-ready figure — flagged **LOW confidence /
UNSOURCED** below. To achieve "vibration," this motor will need a DIY eccentric mass fitted to its
shaft (consistent with "nozzle module... vibration system shakes droplets loose" — this is fabrication,
not a priced BOM line).

**No 5 V coin/ERM alternative was found stocked at bitbyg** (searched; only the RD520PA-class part
appears in the catalogue) — per D-09's bitbyg-default-for-pricing guidance, swapping motors would
require a second vendor and is not recommended purely to solve the drive-scheme question, since a
correctly-driven RD520PA is viable.

**Recommended drive scheme: MOSFET (or transistor) + flyback diode + PWM from the 5 V rail.**

- **Why not a dedicated 3 V rail:** would need a third buck converter/LDO for a burst-only, low-duty
  load — unnecessary complexity and BOM cost for a component that only needs to be "on" for short
  shake bursts.
- **Why not a series resistor:** wastes power as heat proportional to current, and does not allow
  variable-intensity bursts; a PWM'd MOSFET switch is strictly more flexible for the same part count.
- **Why not DRV2605L-class haptic driver** (evaluated per the research brief): DRV2605L
  ([TI product page](https://www.ti.com/product/DRV2605L)) is purpose-built for **ERM/LRA haptic
  actuators** with closed-loop waveform control — it is a mismatch for a generic small brushed motor
  driven open-loop for mechanical shaking, and adds an I²C chip + firmware complexity for no benefit
  here. Not recommended.
- **Recommended part: bitbyg's [IRF520 MOSFET driver module](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/irf520-mosfet-driver-modul/),
  13.75 DKK, HIGH confidence (live page).** Rated 0–24 V load, <5 A (1 A without heatsink, fine for
  this load). **Flag:** the product page and a customer review both note the IRF520 is **not a
  logic-level MOSFET** — its gate threshold is higher than ideal for direct 3.3 V ESP32 GPIO drive,
  meaning it may only partially turn on, running warmer than a proper logic-level part would. Given
  the motor's low estimated current (~100–250 mA class, unsourced), this is likely tolerable in
  practice but should be verified at build time; a logic-level MOSFET or a simple NPN transistor
  (e.g., 2N2222/BC547-class, ubiquitous, not specifically bitbyg-sourced) is a cheap fallback if the
  IRF520 proves unreliable. **MEDIUM confidence** on this recommendation (component choice is sound;
  the specific gate-drive adequacy at 3.3 V for this exact load is unverified).
- **Flyback protection is mandatory** — the motor is inductive; PWM-switching it without a flyback
  (Schottky, e.g., 1N5819-class) across the motor terminals will generate voltage spikes that can
  destroy the MOSFET on every switch-off. State this explicitly in the schema/SPEC — it is a safety
  requirement, not an optional nicety.
- **Pin cost:** 1 PWM-capable GPIO pin. Any ESP32 output-capable GPIO can generate PWM via the LEDC
  peripheral (HIGH confidence, well-documented ESP32 capability) — this does not compete meaningfully
  with the existing pin-budget model; add `+1` to whichever pin-budget line represents "misc.
  peripherals" (or simply note it does not currently exist as a tracked line and should be added).

**BOM impact:** two new component lines — `vibMotor` (RD520PA, €2.35, 17.50 DKK) and a drive-module
line, e.g. `vibDriver` (IRF520 module, €1.84, 13.75 DKK). Both join the shared/fixed block per D-13's
"vibration motor... fixed" instruction.

### Q4 — Capacitive touch-sensor controller

**Chip identified: MPR121** (NOT TTP226/TTP229 as the CONTEXT-SEED speculated). Sourced directly from
the exact bitbyg listing linked in CONTEXT.md
([bitbyg "Kapacitiv Touch Sensor Controller"](https://bitbyg.dk/shop/capacitive-touch-sensor-controller/),
47.50 DKK) — **HIGH confidence, live page fetch**. This is the well-known Freescale/NXP MPR121
proximity-capacitive controller (widely second-sourced by Adafruit/SparkFun breakout boards); its
datasheet is publicly available (e.g.
[SparkFun-hosted MPR121 datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/8/5/c/b/6/MPR121.pdf)).

| Property | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Channels | **12 individual electrodes**, plus 1 simulated 13th (proximity) electrode | High (vendor page) |
| Interface | **I²C** (2-wire serial), default address 0x5A (configurable via ADDR pin to up to 4 addresses) | High |
| Operating voltage | 2.5–3.6 VDC (⚠ NOT 5 V-tolerant — see Q2's rail note) | High |

**Channel count vs 6 containers:** **ONE module is sufficient** — 12 electrodes ≥ 6 containers, with
6 electrodes spare (usable for redundancy, or a second sense-point per container for coarse
two-level detection, at Claude's discretion).

**Interface / bus placement:** Recommend the MPR121 **share the same local I²C attachment already
paid for by the LM75** in the existing pin-budget model. `pinsOf()`'s current comment states: "LM75
onboard temp sensor: shares I²C bus, else +2 dedicated pins" — i.e., if the system bus (Layer B) is
already I²C, LM75 rides for free; otherwise LM75 gets its own dedicated 2-pin local bus. The MPR121
should follow the identical rule and **share that same physical bus/address space as LM75** — its
default address (0x5A) does not collide with LM75's (0x48) — meaning **the capacitive module adds
zero additional pin cost** beyond what LM75 already costs in every existing variant. This is the key
`pinsOf()` extension the planner needs to specify.

**Feasibility as a true liquid-level sensor — flag honestly, do not assume it works.** MPR121 is a
touch/proximity controller, not a purpose-built liquid-level sensor. Community DIY projects do use it
for coarse liquid-level detection by taping metal strips/foil to the *outside* of a non-conductive
container at specific heights and watching the raw 10-bit filtered capacitance value cross a
threshold as liquid rises past each strip — this is a documented, workable technique
([capacitive water-sensor DIY writeup](https://projects.webvoss.de/2021/06/04/simple-capacitive-water-sensor-for-a-water-container/),
NXP community discussion) — **MEDIUM confidence that the *general technique* works**, but:
- It requires the storage containers to be **non-conductive** (plastic/glass) — if Sirio's containers
  are metal, this approach fails outright. **This is an open item — container material is not
  specified anywhere in the read files.**
- Sensitivity depends on container wall thickness, liquid conductivity, and calibration; it is
  fundamentally a **threshold/level-crossing** sensor (like several discrete float switches), not a
  continuous, calibrated volume gauge — appropriate for "container is low/empty" alerts, not precise
  volume readout.
- Ambient humidity and nearby-object proximity can skew readings (sourced from the general
  capacitive-sensing literature above) — real device (not lab-bench) reliability is unverified.

**Recommend flagging this explicitly as an open risk/assumption in the schema's UI/Storage module
notes** (D-04's payload chip for "capacitive sensing ×6 containers" is accurate as a *component*
claim, but the sentence "DIY capacitive volume-level detection" in the CONTEXT-SEED overstates
feasibility — it is level-crossing detection at best, and container material is a precondition that
is currently unverified).

**BOM impact:** one new component line, e.g. `capTouch` (MPR121 module, €6.37, 47.50 DKK), joins the
shared/fixed block per Q2's rail-topology extension of D-13's grouping (Claude's Discretion, flagged

### Q5 — Temperature compensation (note only, no modelling)

**Viscosity change dominates volumetric thermal expansion for water-like liquids near room
temperature, by roughly two orders of magnitude.** Both figures below are well-established physical
constants — **HIGH confidence**, cross-referenced against a reputable engineering reference:

- **Volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of water** ≈ 0.0002–0.0004 /°C (≈0.02–0.04 %/°C) near
  room temperature ([engineeringtoolbox.com — Volumetric (Cubic) Thermal Expansion](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/volumetric-temperature-expansion-d_315.html)).
  This directly drives Sirio's "dispensed-volume" worry: a fixed-volume dispense mechanism (like a
  peristaltic pump metering by displacement) delivers a very slightly different *mass* of liquid as
  its density shifts with temperature — but the effect is small (well under 0.1% for a few °C swing).
- **Dynamic viscosity of water** drops from ≈1.00 mPa·s at 20 °C to ≈0.89 mPa·s at 25 °C
  ([engineeringtoolbox.com — Water Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/water-dynamic-kinematic-viscosity-d_596.html))
  — an ≈11 % change over 5 °C, i.e. **≈2–2.5 %/°C** near room temperature. For a peristaltic pump,
  viscosity affects how completely the tube re-fills between roller strokes and the flow resistance
  through the nozzle — this is the effect that actually dominates dose-to-dose repeatability drift
  with temperature for water-like liquids.

**Recommendation for the one-line UI note (D-17, wording is Claude's discretion):** lead with
viscosity as the physically dominant effect, and frame thermal volume expansion as the secondary,
much smaller effect — e.g. something like *"Ambient temperature affects dispensed volume mainly
through liquid viscosity (a few % per °C); the liquid's own thermal expansion is a much smaller,
secondary effect."* No hardware, no cost impact, no modelling — this is a documentation-only finding
for the planner to turn into exact copy.
