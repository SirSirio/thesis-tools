5. **Power-rail model (SC-5) — a new section, since the current model only ever sized the PSU for
   driver/motor concurrency and never accounted for logic, alignment, or screen current.** Document:
   - **28BYJ-48: the 12 V variant is the researched choice (D-15).** ~60 mA/phase (200 Ω winding) vs
     ~83 mA/phase for the 5 V/60 Ω winding, and ≥34.3 mN·m vs ≥29.4 mN·m pull-in torque — less
     current *and* more torque, at the identical 25 DKK price on the same bitbyg listing dropdown.
     No trade-off. **High** confidence (manufacturer datasheet + live vendor page).
   - **Topology: one 24 V PSU output, two local buck converters** — not a wall-supplied "12 V + 24 V
     dual rail". 24 V → drivers/NEMA17 (unchanged); 24 V → LM2596 (trimmed to 12 V) → 2× 28BYJ-48;
     24 V → fixed 5 V/5 A synchronous buck → ESP32 + screen + SD + LM75 + MPR121 + vibration drive.
   - **The arithmetic**, written out: 12 V rail ≈ 0.24 A load → 2.88 W out → ≈3.39 W in at ~85 %
     buck efficiency → ≈**0.14 A** off 24 V. 5 V rail ≈ 0.78 A nominal / ~1.1 A transient → ≈5 W out
     → ≈5.88 W in → ≈**0.25 A** off 24 V. **Total new draw ≈ 0.39 A ≈ 9.4 W** — negligible against
     the 60 W/150 W PSU sizing, so **no PSU wattage change is required**.
   - **Confidence: Medium**, and say why: the rail choices and buck sourcing are High, but the
     ESP32/screen/SD load figures are typical datasheet-class estimates, not measurements on the
     specific bitbyg parts (RESEARCH Assumption A2). The RD520PA's contribution is unsourced
     entirely. Flag the ESP32 dev board's onboard 3V3-regulator rating as an unsourced build-time
     check (RESEARCH Risk #4) — very likely fine given MPR121's few-mA draw, but not a fact.
   - **Why 7805-class linear regulation was rejected:** at 24 V → 5 V with a 0.5 A load the regulator
     dissipates (24−5)×0.5 = **9.5 W** as heat in an enclosed device — thermally infeasible without a
     large heatsink, and wastes ~80 % of input power vs a buck's ~85–92 % efficiency. This follows
     from P = ΔV × I, not a vendor claim.
   - **Why DRV2605L-class haptic drivers were rejected:** purpose-built for closed-loop ERM/LRA
     haptics, a mismatch for open-loop mechanical shaking of a generic brushed motor; adds an I²C
     chip and firmware for no benefit.
