A pipette and a bench aren’t always there.

Most diagnostic assays assume a lab: a technician, a pipette, a bench. Point-of-care testing removes all three — the device itself has to get the liquid handling right, unsupervised, wherever the patient actually is.

The requirements are unforgiving on purpose: dispense five or more different liquids, from 5 µL to 1000 µL, accurate and reproducible to within ±10% — and every wetted part has to be cleanable or disposable, with no spill and no contact with the operator. That’s not a chemistry problem. It’s a mechanical design problem, and it’s the one this thesis is about.

Six modules, one job each.

Storage holds the liquids and watches their level. Pump meters each one out by roller displacement. Nozzle shakes a droplet loose in short bursts. Alignment moves the sample rack underneath. Software & Electronics is the brain and the bus, sealed away from anything wet. UI is where a protocol gets set — on a laptop, loaded in from an SD card — and where the run gets watched.

Every prototype is a corrected mistake.

Proto-01 under-dispensed by about a third against its target — a real, measured gap. Proto-02 corrects the rotor geometry that caused it, and whatever proto-02 gets wrong will shape the version after that. None of it moves in a straight line.

Peristaltic Rotor Geometry Solver

Analytically solves rotor radius for peristaltic pumps. Set your target stroke volume, tube ID, roller bearing, and occlusion efficiency — get a full geometry table for 3–12 rollers with feasibility checks.

Peristaltic Occlusion & Displaced-Volume Model

Two-part geometric reference for tube-deformation mechanics. Part 1: stadium cross-section model with interactive SVG — residual lumen area vs. roller squeeze. Part 2: axial contact length, displaced volume per roller pass, and arc compensation formula. Full derivations with LaTeX formulas.

Open tool→
〰

Tensioned Tube-Path & Stroke Volume Model

Alternative path geometry for a tube pinned at both ends under seating tension — straight roller-to-roller runs with α/2 wraps instead of the 180° wall arc. Includes axial pre-strain corrections (lumen shrink, interference loss), volume-per-stroke prediction, and a closed-form corrected rotor radius.
