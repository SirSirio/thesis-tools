Reagent vial

The liquid starts in an inverted 4 mL glass vial with a septum; a capacitive strip reads the level.

Six vials sit along the rear of the machine, one per channel. The storage module is the companion thesis by Marius Schiller; this page follows the liquid from the moment it leaves the vial.

Pump head

Four rollers on a printed rotor meter by geometry: one rotor step is one stroke, so a dose is a step count.

Four builds. The first delivered 3.39 µL of a nominal 5 µL, which triggered a full characterisation of the printer. The fourth build hit its 1.52 mm gap and was qualified on the balance.

Needle and release

Six blunt needles seat on their moulded Luer hubs, so any gauge swaps in without tools; a vibration burst shakes the pendant droplet free.

The carrier is one printed part. An eccentric-mass motor sits in it, and a short burst after each dose is enough to release the droplet into the tube below.

Rack under the needle

A printed rack and pinion indexes eight tubes at 22 mm pitch under the fixed needles; the finished rack is ejected by a motorless fishbone cam.

The stage is also the chassis, printed in three dovetail-keyed sections. About fifty concepts were sketched; one was built, then given a homing switch and a second axis to feed the queue.

The screen that ordered it

A 3.2-inch resistive touchscreen with a tethered stylus; recipes, tube assignment across five racks, a pre-run check that refuses to start short.

Every candidate interface was drawn as a self-auditing web page at the panel's native resolution before any firmware existed, so the chosen design was already tested for touch targets and contrast.

One clock for six pumps

One ESP32-S3 and commodity step/direction drivers: a shared step and direction line, one enable per driver, eight pins instead of twelve.

The same controller sequences the motors and drives the screen. Twenty-five candidate architectures were costed and pin-audited before this one was wired on a breadboard.

Design, build, test, learn. Per module.

Prototypes

Four pump builds and about fifty alignment concepts. Every measurement was gravimetric, on a 0.1 mg balance, with the method adapted from ISO 23783-2.

CAD and printing

Fully parametric Fusion 360. The printer was characterised like an instrument: ±0.10 mm, outer dimensions shrink about 0.65 %, bores lose 0.14 mm.

AI in the loop

Claude Code in VS Code, through a spec-driven workflow, for the firmware and these tools. An MCP link to Fusion 360 verified CAD dimensions against the specs; assembly generation failed and stayed manual.
