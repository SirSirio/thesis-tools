Proto 01 · Built 2026

5 µL 4-roller peristaltic — baseline

Purpose

The first physical build — and its job was never to hit spec. It was a minimum viable prototype with three goals: prove the concept (can a peristaltic pump be built in this modular, 3D-printed, point-of-care form and actually move liquid?), validate the model (is the geometry model I built correct?), and learn before committing to the next design cycle. On all three it delivered: it pumps, the model predicts the messy reality to ~11 %, and it exposed three concrete, fixable design errors.

The design calculation, worked

The chain the two tools ran, shown end-to-end. (A = lumen area; Lc = one roller's axial squeeze length; ΔArc = extra arc swept to compensate tube deformation.)

A = π(d/2)² = π(0.51/2)² = 0.2043 mm² L_c = k·2√(2·R_r·δ) = 1.15·2√(2·5·0.20) = 3.253 mm ΔArc= N_c·L_c = 1·3.253 = 3.253 mm ← N_c=1 (error: should be 2) arc = vol/A + ΔArc = 5.0/0.2043 + 3.253 = 27.73 mm gVol= arc·A = 27.73·0.2043 = 5.66 µL (gross sweep) R = N·arc/(2π) = 4·27.73/(2π) = 17.70 mm (rotor, as built)
Is 5.66 µL out of place? No — it is the gross sweep. The extra 0.66 µL is the volume the model expects to lose to tube deformation; net = 5.0 µL. It is a valid reference because that loss is real.

What actually happened — and why it under-delivered

The gap was wrong. The tool prescribes the gap as G = 2w − δ = 1.50 mm. The part was built at 1.75 mm. But the tube fully closes only once the gap drops below "walls-kiss" = 2w = 1.70 mm. At 1.75 mm > 1.70 mm, the rollers never occluded the tube at all — so it could not pump.

The paper-shim fix. To make it run, paper was folded into the groove so the tube sat proud. Caliper on the folded paper: ≈0.78 mm (very hard) up to ≈1.1 mm (light) — turning the effective gap into roughly 0.9–1.1 mm, i.e. an effective interference δeff ≈ 0.6–0.8 mm (a heavy over-squeeze).

Firm shim → δ_eff = 0.80 mm, with the TRUE N_c = 2: L_c(0.80) = 1.15·2√(2·5·0.80) = 6.505 mm ΔArc = 2·6.505 = 13.01 mm net = (arc_built − ΔArc)·A = (27.73 − 13.01)·0.2043 = 3.01 µL
Model validated. Predicted 3.01 µL (firm shim); measured 3.39 µL (gravimetric). Back-solving from 3.39 gives an effective gap of 1.11 mm — exactly the light-shim band that was measured. The first-order model brackets a hand-folded paper-shim experiment to ~11 %: how the system was modelled is correct.

Wall thickness — a real measurement problem

w is the highest-leverage input (it sets the gap via G = 2w − δ) and the hardest to pin down: the exact tube's datasheet doesn't cleanly state it, and a caliper is unreliable because the soft tube compresses under the jaws. We used w ≈ 0.85 mm as a best estimate. That the pump delivered anything before reliable shimming hints w may be slightly above 0.85 mm. Proto-02 must measure w properly (microscope cross-section, or micrometer OD then w = (OD − d)/2). This is a documented model limitation.

Noise & vibration test (firmware)

Proto-01 was very noisy with strong vibration on the DRV8825 driver in full step. A purpose-built bench tool (vibration_test.cpp) allowed live sweeping of microstepping × speed over serial without re-flashing. On a DRV8825 there is no "smoothing library" — the levers are the acceleration ramp (already handled) and microstepping.

Bench-tested best noise-vs-torque compromise on the existing DRV8825 — quiet and smooth enough while keeping torque margin (1/4-step's torque penalty is small, so step-skip risk under the 2-roller load stays low). A silent TMC2209/2226 driver is the fallback if more silence is later needed, but it costs torque on this torque-hungry, open-loop pump. Finding: full step is the noise source; 1/4 step at ~180 RPM is the recommended operating point — free, no new parts.

Issues observed

  • Tube not squeezed enough — gap too wide; needed a paper shim to function at all (the headline mechanical failure).
  • Under-delivery — ≈3.4 µL vs 5.0 µL target, −32 %.
  • Pump head not fixed — had to be held down by hand during delivery; no locking mechanism.
  • Wobble — 0.25 mm loose tolerance let the head move, and head position is the gap.
  • Noisy / vibration — full-step DRV8825 (addressed above).

→ Inputs for Proto-02

This is the design brief the next prototype carries forward.

Geometry

  • Fix Nc = 2 in the displaced-volume model → re-derive ΔArc → rotor grows ~17.7 → ~19.7 mm.
  • Carry the tool's gap into CAD. Print a sweep of pump heads (G ≈ 1.35 / 1.50 / 1.65 mm) → maps gap → output and reveals the true w.

Tube installation (3 points)

  • Reposition holders so the tube sits under minimal stretch.
  • Tighten the holder holes so the tube won't pop out once seated.
  • Add a shield/retainer so the tube can't fall behind the rollers.

Head fixation & fit

  • Add a positive lock (screw clamp, or cam/lever) — makes the gap repeatable and the head sweep measurable. Not optional.
  • Reduce loose-fit tolerance 0.25 → ~0.10 mm, paired with the lock.

Firmware & calibration

  • Adopt 1/4 microstep @ ~2400 steps/s (180 RPM); recalibrate steps/stroke.
  • Don't change k yet — proto-01 mixes two error sources; back-calculate k from a clean proto-02 measurement. Measure w first.

Why 4 rollers

Selected over higher counts despite continuous-flow practice recommending 8–10 rollers for low-flow accuracy. The distinction is operational mode: those recommendations target continuous metering, where more rollers reduce pulsation. This system does discrete stop-start dosing, where each roller hand-off (one releasing as the next engages) is an error event at the stop. Fewer rollers → fewer hand-offs per dispense → fewer stop-event errors — the opposite of the continuous-flow heuristic.

Honest caveat. Proto-01's data can't yet demonstrate this — the −32 % systematic occlusion error swamps the hand-off signature. Proto-02 is the first build where the roller-count rationale becomes testable.
