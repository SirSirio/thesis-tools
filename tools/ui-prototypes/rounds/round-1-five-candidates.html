01Why these constraints, and where they come from

Every number below was measured from the actual build or read off a primary source. Where a widely-repeated figure turned out to be folklore, it is named as such — a thesis that cites a standard which does not say what it is claimed to say is worse than one that cites nothing.

ConstraintValueWhere it comes from
44 px targets≈ 9.0 mm at 125 DPI Not from WCAG. WCAG 2.5.5 (AAA) says 44 CSS px ≈ 11.6 mm; WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) says only 24. The 9 mm figure is the convergent empirical band — Material Design (48 dp ≈ 9 mm), Microsoft WinUI (7.5 mm), Parhi et al. 2006 (9.2 mm discrete / 9.6 mm serial), ANSI/HFES 100 (9.5 mm). On this panel 9 mm happens to land on 44 px.
Gloved use20 mm = 98 px MIL-STD-1472H: 15 mm minimum, +5 mm for gloves. At 98 px this panel fits nine targets total. Gloved operation is not achievable on a 3.2″ touchscreen — it needs a physical control or a bigger panel. Stated as a limit, not designed around.
≥ 7:1 contrastWCAG 2.x AAA Chosen over AA (4.5:1) because ambient glare only ever subtracts. Measured after RGB565 quantisation, not before.
No gestures— Resistive single-point. Swipe and multi-touch are not reliably available; every function is a discrete press.
Flat fills only— RGB565 gives green 64 levels and red/blue 32 — LVGL publishes a dithering tutorial precisely because smooth gradients band. A hardware limit that happens to agree with current visual language.

02What the renderer will actually do

The LVGL 9.5 source was read directly rather than trusting the docs site. Every widget these mockups use exists and is enabled by default. Three things were banned from the mockups because they are affordable in CSS and not on this target: box and drop shadows (LV_DRAW_SW_SHADOW_CACHE_SIZE is 0 in this project, so every shadowed rectangle is recomputed every redraw), blur and backdrop blur, and rotation or scaling of widgets. Radial and conical gradients are also out — they need LV_USE_DRAW_SW_COMPLEX_GRADIENTS, which is off.

The frame budget is arithmetic, not opinion: 320 × 240 × 2 bytes = 153,600 B; over SPI at 40 MHz that is 30.7 ms of wire time, a 32.5 fps ceiling before a single pixel is rendered. Realistically 15–20 fps full-screen. So animation is confined to regions: a bar filling, a value tweening, a button's press state. No full-screen transitions in any candidate.

One free win, independent of which design is chosen: the draw buffer is currently 320 × 10 lines (6.4 KB), single-buffered — 24 separate transfers per full redraw. Espressif's own measurements put an internal-RAM DMA buffer at 28 fps against 12 fps for a PSRAM one. Two 320 × 40 buffers in internal RAM with DMA is the single biggest smoothness change available, and it is a build-config edit.

03Sunlight: the honest answer

The fitted panel cannot be made sun-readable by any design choice, and the arithmetic says so plainly. A diffuse reflector's luminance is L = R·E/π. At 100,000 lux with an 8% effective stack reflectance — a resistive overlay's air gap is structural and cannot be bonded away — reflected luminance is 2,546 cd/m². The panel emits ~200 cd/m², of which a resistive layer transmits about 80%, so 160 cd/m² reaches the eye.

Ambient contrast ratio is therefore (160 + 2546) / 2546 = 1.06 : 1, against an industry-convention minimum of 5:1. Because reflected light adds to numerator and denominator alike, no rendering choice can subtract it — the entire achievable palette spans 6% of contrast. Reaching 5:1 at this reflectance would need ~10,200 nits. Optical bonding drops reflectance to 0.5%, at which point 1000 nits gives 7.3:1 — which is exactly where the industry's "800–1000 nits = sunlight readable" convention comes from, and it is conditional on bonding. This panel is off that curve by roughly 50×.

What design does buy is bright-ambient and shade legibility. Dark-on-light is kept in every candidate for that reason: in glare the screen's own reflection washes out dark pixels while white ones still read, so dark mode is strictly worse outdoors. 04The stop button question

Worth settling before choosing a layout, because it changes what must be on screen. Across commercial liquid handlers, enclosure interlock plus software stop is the dominant pattern, not a hardwired emergency stop. Tecan Fluent's 199-page manual contains zero occurrences of the word "emergency"; Eppendorf epMotion the same; Hamilton STAR lists an E-stop box only in its ordering appendix; Opentrons OT-2 has software stop only. Opentrons Flex is the exception that mandates one.

So a well-designed touchscreen abort is defensible for a benchtop prototype — but it earns that only by being always present and always reachable. That is why every candidate here keeps abort on screen during a run rather than behind navigation, and why the machine's energised state is persistent chrome rather than a screen you visit.

05What the current interface does, measured

The inherited UI was audited line by line before any of this was drawn. It was a competent handoff under deadline and the port deliberately did not refactor it — but the redesign is not a matter of taste alone. The audit found a cold-boot deadlock (containers default to "NA", which forces run-eligibility false, so a fresh device cannot start any protocol until every liquid is configured), a calibration screen with no exit when the sensor is absent (the back button is hidden on entry and progress only advances from a callback that returns early without the sensor — the only escape is a reboot), and an abort that tells motion nothing (it sets a flag and navigates; the pumps keep running).

Two displayed values are fiction: a four-block battery indicator on a device with no battery and no fuel gauge, permanently reading 75%; and a hardcoded 5 µL/s flow rate driving every time estimate, while real dosing is step-count based at 4.56 µL/stroke. Level percentages are also shown as a flat 100% when no sensor answers, and are then used as a volume gate — the one thing SENSE-02 forbids.

The smallest interactive element is 25 × 14 px (the protocol switch), with 0 px between adjacent rows. Seven of twelve control types fall under 44 × 44. This is what the ⊞ Targets overlay is calibrated against.

06How to read these

Pick a candidate on the left, step through its screens, then turn on the overlays. The audit panel is computed from the same data that draws the pixels, so it cannot flatter the design. What is being asked of you is not "which is prettiest" but which model of the machine is right — A, B and C are three dresses on roughly one idea; D and E disagree with that idea and with each other.
