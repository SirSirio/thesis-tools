How the three differ

F — Instrument. A dense, calm lab panel. Persistent 26 px status strip, a six-channel level card as the centre of gravity, three actions in a row. Closest to the instruments the operator already knows.

Bet: least relearning, most information per screen. Cost: the home screen is a menu with a readout attached, not a picture of the machine.

G — Guided. Every task is a numbered wizard under a persistent step spine. Home is three large action rows. Largest targets, fewest elements per screen, most hand-holding.

Bet: an operator who has never seen the machine can finish a run. Cost: slower for the expert, and levels are one tap away rather than present.

H — Workbench. Home is the machine, drawn. The rack lane runs across the panel with six nozzles above it, each carrying its channel colour and level. The same drawing becomes the clearance diagram and the run view.

Bet: "where is my rack and what is in the lines" is answered without reading. Cost: the most drawing, and the most coupled to real geometry.

Honesty rules visible in the pixels

  • Volume is quantised to the sensor's real resolution. 40 mL container → 1 mL steps, 15 mL → 0.5 mL, 4 mL → 0.2 mL. Under 5% it reads < 2 mL, not a false figure.
  • Three distinct unknown states. — no sensor, — not calibrated, — not configured. The old UI showed a flat 100% for all three and then gated the run on it.
  • Every time estimate is derived from step counts. The hardcoded flowrate = 5 µL/s is deleted. Pumps share one clock, so a multi-liquid dose takes max(Vᵢ)/54.72 seconds — never the sum.
  • The battery is a designed placeholder that renders not fitted. There is no battery and no rail sensing in this build; it never shows a level.
  • "Commanded, not measured" appears on the finish screen. Volumes are open-loop step counts.
