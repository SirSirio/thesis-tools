What all three proposals change beside colour

The label sits at the very top of the tile and the sub-line at the very bottom — ui_btn() places them at y = 6 and y = h − 20, so a 72 px tile has a 28 px hole in the middle and the label reads as pushed up. All three proposals centre the label + sub-line pair as one block instead (label at y = 19, sub at y = 40), and give the title the same vertical centre as the gear beside it. That is a two-line change in ui_widgets.cpp and it applies to every tile-style button in the interface at once.

Constraints every proposal respects

  • One solid accent per screen. "Start a run" stays the only fully filled tile, so it keeps the hierarchy you liked.
  • No state by colour alone (UX-02) — the Liquids tile keeps its icon and its word; colour only reinforces them.
  • Alarm states are untouched. Amber (getting low) and solid red (needs attention) render exactly as the firmware does today in all four panels — softening the alarm to fit a palette would be the wrong trade.
  • No gradients, no shadows, no new fonts. Everything here is a flat rectangle or a circle, i.e. things LVGL already draws for free at this frame rate.
  • Colours are taken from the palette the product already has — the ColorBrewer Dark2 channel set (CH[]) and its light tints (CHBG_L[]) — so no new tokens need a contrast audit, only a re-check of ink on the tint.

Firmware cost, roughly

  • A — Colour rails: one extra ui_rect() per tile plus an icon colour. Smallest change.
  • B — Tinted cards: new BtnTone values (or a tint field on BtnOpt); no new drawing at all. Needs an ink-on-tint contrast check.
  • C — Channel ribbon + discs: six small rects for the ribbon, one circle per tile, and a left-offset change to the label column. Largest change of the three, still small.
