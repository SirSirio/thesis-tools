Design Tool · Peristaltic Pump Qualification

Pump Testing Protocol

The gravimetric method used to qualify the peristaltic dispensing pump, adapted from ISO 23783-2, with a calibrated micropipette benchmark in the manner of ISO 8655.

01

Method in five lines

  1. Balance. Readability 0.1 mg. The pump delivers a known number of strokes into a vessel on the balance, and the mass gain is the measurement.
  2. Evaporation. Measured as a blank before the runs at 0.118 mg/s, then applied to every reading in proportion to that dispense duration. The correction stays under 0.7 % of the reading.
  3. Order. The run sequence is fully randomised, so tube wear and session drift cannot alias onto speed or stroke count.
  4. Mass to volume. A single Z factor, in µL/mg, converts each mass reading:
    $$V_i \;=\; m_i \cdot Z$$
  5. Benchmark. A calibrated micropipette is weighed on the same balance, by the same method, interleaved with the pump, so the instrument error cancels in the comparison.
02

What was run on proto-02

  1. Hardware: proto-02 v2.3, four rollers, occlusion gap set to 1.52 mm, 0.51 mm inner diameter tube, fresh and pre-wetted.
  2. Full factorial: 1, 100 and 300 strokes crossed with 60, 120, 180 and 240 rpm.
  3. 76 replicates, no acquisition failures.
  4. Deionised water at about 20 °C and 20 % relative humidity.
  5. The micropipette reference weighed in the same session, on the same balance.
03

Results

4.53 µLper stroke at 180 rpm
0.25 %delivered volume CV, 100 strokes
0.34 %delivered volume CV, 300 strokes
0.27 %micropipette CV, same balance
2 %monotonic droop, 60 to 240 rpm
~6 %single 5 µL stroke CV, upper bound

On multi stroke doses the pump is as consistent as the calibrated micropipette it is meant to replace, and the volume it delivers per stroke is a stable, predictable offset from the 5 µL nominal that a step count multiplier corrects.

The single stroke figure is an upper bound imposed by the 0.1 mg balance rather than a measured value: separating true stroke to stroke scatter from session drift needs a finer balance or a photometric method.

04

Deviations from the ideal

The standard describes a certification bench. This is a prototype bench. Every departure is recorded with its reason and its cost, which is a stronger position than claiming a compliance the hardware does not support.

DeviationWhyEffect
Balance of 0.1 mg instead of the 0.001 mg the 0.5 to 20 µL band asks for No semi micro balance available at prototype stage Per stroke volume is read from the slope of a multi stroke series, and single dose precision is bounded rather than measured
3 to 5 evaporation blanks per session instead of at least 10 Session time, and a fast weigh keeps the vessel exposed for only a few seconds Correction stays below 0.7 % of every reading
Pre rinse of at least 5 flushes kept in full Costs nothing and removes the run to run non stationarity seen in early sessions No deviation, the one requirement kept as written
Room temperature noted at start and end instead of a 2 hour equilibration with logged temperature, humidity and pressure Open bench, no climate control Drift is flagged, not quantified
Z factor taken from water density alone, with no buoyancy term Buoyancy moves the result by under 0.3 % Negligible against the reported coefficients of variation
3, 5 or 10 replicates by volume instead of 10 at every point Prototype means, not certification figures Wider confidence interval on the mean, widest at the single stroke point

The build, the gap sweep and the full result tables live in the Proto-02 record. The rotor geometry this protocol qualifies was set with the Rotor Geometry Solver.
