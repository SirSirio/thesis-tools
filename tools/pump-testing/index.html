01

Framing — two standards, two jobs

Two stacked layers

This page documents a dispensing-accuracy qualification for the peristaltic pump as two stacked layers. The layer you are reading now is the top layer — the market-grade "righteous" protocol: how this qualification should be run if the pump went to market, grounded in the applicable metrology standards. Sections 2–7 specify the dispensing-accuracy core in full depth; Sections 8–9 map the surrounding methods landscape and the wider go-to-market picture more lightly. The bottom layer — the actual method used on the prototype, with the reasoned justification for each deviation from this restrictive ideal — lives in Section 10, Actual prototype protocol & deviations. That layer is authored separately (Plan 09-06); this plan establishes the top layer and the slot it fills into.

The dual-frame verdict

Which standard governs a qualification of a modular, point-of-care peristaltic dispensing pump is not a single-standard question. Two standards apply, but they do two different jobs, and neither is the other's substitute:

Test method

ISO 23783-2 Annex D — the single-channel gravimetric procedure. The pump is, literally, an automated liquid handling system (ALHS) by the standard's own scope statement (§1): "applicable to all ALHS… which perform liquid handling tasks without human intervention." Annex D is written for exactly this device shape — one channel, any liquid, contact delivery into a vessel — so it supplies how to measure: apparatus, preparation, environmental control, evaporation handling, mass→volume conversion.

Performance benchmark

ISO 8655 (Parts 2 and 6) — the piston-operated-pipette accuracy/precision standard, applied here by analogy, not by literal compliance claim (the pump is not a piston-operated apparatus). The device's job is to replace a person pipetting by hand, so pipette-grade trueness/CV is the natural "what good looks like" comparison — it supplies what counts as accurate.

An honest correction: Part 2 does not set device tolerances

ISO 23783-2 Part 2 is a test method standard, not a device specification. Its own Reporting clause (§9) states that "measurement results, traceability, and measurement uncertainty shall be reported in accordance with ISO 23783-3" — the part of the family that would set ALHS-native pass/fail tolerances. ISO 23783-3 is not in this project's possession and its content is not verified here; that gap is named honestly rather than paraphrased (see Section 7).

Table 1 of ISO 23783-2 (an overview of methods, §5.1) lists typical systematic/random error figures achievable by the measurement method itself — a measurement-uncertainty budget for the test, not a specification for the device. Reading "the gravimetric method can resolve to 1.4% systematic / 0.6% random at 0.5–20 µL" as "the device must be accurate to 1.4%/0.6%" would be a category error this protocol deliberately avoids (see Section 6 for where the method-uncertainty and device-benchmark numbers are kept clearly separate). Because Part 2 supplies no device-level tolerance and Part 3 is unavailable, the "what good performance looks like" number has to come from ISO 8655 instead — the benchmark frame above.

02

Gravimetric test method — ISO 23783-2 Annex D

Apparatus & test liquid

The reference method is single-channel gravimetric weighing (Annex D): the pump delivers a known number of strokes into a vessel resting on an analytical balance, and the mass gain is converted to volume. The test liquid is ISO 3696 grade 3 water — the standard reference liquid for gravimetric dispensing verification, chosen for its well-characterised, temperature- dependent density (see the Tanaka formula in Section 4).

Preparation (§6.3)

  • Pre-rinse the delivery path ≥5× to waste on every tip/tube change, before any measurement cycle begins.
  • Bring the pump, test liquid, and balance to thermal equilibrium (±2 °C) for ≥2 hours before testing starts.
  • Allow the balance to settle for ≥6 seconds before reading each mass (§6.1).

Environmental conditions — Table D.1

ISO 23783-2 Annex D, Table D.1 — test conditions for the gravimetric method. These are the gravimetric-method-specific refinement of §6.3's general ±1 °C / ±5% RH in-test tolerance.
Environmental conditionRangePermitted change during test
Water temperature17 °C – 30 °C≤ 1 °C

Evaporation-blank workflow (§D.5.2)

Before any test-liquid measurements begin, the method requires ≥10 blank measurement cycles — empty dispense/weigh cycles run at the same cycle time as a real delivery — to characterise the evaporation rate of the open vessel under the actual test conditions. This blank baseline is subtracted from subsequent test-liquid measurements (formulas D.1–D.2) so that evaporation loss during the weighing interval is not mistaken for a dispensing error.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 Annex D (single-channel gravimetric method), §6.3 (general preparation), §6.1 (balance settle time), Table D.1 (environmental conditions), §D.5.2 (evaporation blank).

03

Balance requirements — Table 3

Minimum balance requirements by delivered volume

ISO 23783-2's Table 3 ("Minimum requirements for balances for weighing liquids") sets the balance grade required to gravimetrically verify a given delivered volume. The row that applies is determined purely by the volume being delivered per measurement — not by the device technology — so it applies to this pump exactly as it would to any single-channel dispenser.

ISO 23783-2 Table 3. Note (a): assumes one delivery of test liquid from a single channel. Note (b): expanded uncertainty in use (k=2) may be determined per ASTM E898-20 / EURAMET CG-18 at the largest volume in the range.
Delivered volumeReadabilityRepeatabilityExpanded uncertainty in use (k=2)
< 0.5 µL0.0001 mg0.0005 mg0.001 mg
0.5 ≤ V < 20 µL0.001 mg0.006 mg0.012 mg
20 ≤ V < 200 µL0.01 mg0.025 mg0.05 mg
200 ≤ V ≤ 10 000 µL0.1 mg0.2 mg0.4 mg

The pump's ~5 µL/stroke target falls in the highlighted 0.5–20 µL band, which requires a 0.001 mg (1 µg, semi-micro) balance — the compliant requirement for this device.

Where the prototype stands

The prototype's actual balance resolves to 0.1 mg — about two orders of magnitude coarser than the 0.001 mg this band requires. This is the single binding metrological constraint on the prototype's test campaign, and the workarounds used to test under it (slope method, per-stroke CV via √N, same-instrument pipette comparison) are documented with their full justification in Section 10, Actual prototype protocol & deviations.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 Table 3 (minimum requirements for balances).
