01

Framing — two standards, two jobs

Two stacked layers

This page documents a dispensing-accuracy qualification for the peristaltic pump as two stacked layers. The layer you are reading now is the top layer — the market-grade "righteous" protocol: how this qualification should be run if the pump went to market, grounded in the applicable metrology standards. Sections 2–7 specify the dispensing-accuracy core in full depth; Sections 8–9 map the surrounding methods landscape and the wider go-to-market picture more lightly. The bottom layer — the actual method used on the prototype, with the reasoned justification for each deviation from this restrictive ideal — lives in Section 10, Actual prototype protocol & deviations. That layer is authored separately (Plan 09-06); this plan establishes the top layer and the slot it fills into.

The dual-frame verdict

Which standard governs a qualification of a modular, point-of-care peristaltic dispensing pump is not a single-standard question. Two standards apply, but they do two different jobs, and neither is the other's substitute:

Test method

ISO 23783-2 Annex D — the single-channel gravimetric procedure. The pump is, literally, an automated liquid handling system (ALHS) by the standard's own scope statement (§1): "applicable to all ALHS… which perform liquid handling tasks without human intervention." Annex D is written for exactly this device shape — one channel, any liquid, contact delivery into a vessel — so it supplies how to measure: apparatus, preparation, environmental control, evaporation handling, mass→volume conversion.

Performance benchmark

ISO 8655 (Parts 2 and 6) — the piston-operated-pipette accuracy/precision standard, applied here by analogy, not by literal compliance claim (the pump is not a piston-operated apparatus). The device's job is to replace a person pipetting by hand, so pipette-grade trueness/CV is the natural "what good looks like" comparison — it supplies what counts as accurate.

An honest correction: Part 2 does not set device tolerances

ISO 23783-2 Part 2 is a test method standard, not a device specification. Its own Reporting clause (§9) states that "measurement results, traceability, and measurement uncertainty shall be reported in accordance with ISO 23783-3" — the part of the family that would set ALHS-native pass/fail tolerances. ISO 23783-3 is not in this project's possession and its content is not verified here; that gap is named honestly rather than paraphrased (see Section 7).

Table 1 of ISO 23783-2 (an overview of methods, §5.1) lists typical systematic/random error figures achievable by the measurement method itself — a measurement-uncertainty budget for the test, not a specification for the device. Reading "the gravimetric method can resolve to 1.4% systematic / 0.6% random at 0.5–20 µL" as "the device must be accurate to 1.4%/0.6%" would be a category error this protocol deliberately avoids (see Section 6 for where the method-uncertainty and device-benchmark numbers are kept clearly separate). Because Part 2 supplies no device-level tolerance and Part 3 is unavailable, the "what good performance looks like" number has to come from ISO 8655 instead — the benchmark frame above.

02

Gravimetric test method — ISO 23783-2 Annex D

Apparatus & test liquid

The reference method is single-channel gravimetric weighing (Annex D): the pump delivers a known number of strokes into a vessel resting on an analytical balance, and the mass gain is converted to volume. The test liquid is ISO 3696 grade 3 water — the standard reference liquid for gravimetric dispensing verification, chosen for its well-characterised, temperature- dependent density (see the Tanaka formula in Section 4).

Preparation (§6.3)

  • Pre-rinse the delivery path ≥5× to waste on every tip/tube change, before any measurement cycle begins.
  • Bring the pump, test liquid, and balance to thermal equilibrium (±2 °C) for ≥2 hours before testing starts.
  • Allow the balance to settle for ≥6 seconds before reading each mass (§6.1).

Environmental conditions — Table D.1

ISO 23783-2 Annex D, Table D.1 — test conditions for the gravimetric method. These are the gravimetric-method-specific refinement of §6.3's general ±1 °C / ±5% RH in-test tolerance.

Evaporation-blank workflow (§D.5.2)

Before any test-liquid measurements begin, the method requires ≥10 blank measurement cycles — empty dispense/weigh cycles run at the same cycle time as a real delivery — to characterise the evaporation rate of the open vessel under the actual test conditions. This blank baseline is subtracted from subsequent test-liquid measurements (formulas D.1–D.2) so that evaporation loss during the weighing interval is not mistaken for a dispensing error.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 Annex D (single-channel gravimetric method), §6.3 (general preparation), §6.1 (balance settle time), Table D.1 (environmental conditions), §D.5.2 (evaporation blank).

Balance requirements — Table 3

Minimum balance requirements by delivered volume

ISO 23783-2's Table 3 ("Minimum requirements for balances for weighing liquids") sets the balance grade required to gravimetrically verify a given delivered volume. The row that applies is determined purely by the volume being delivered per measurement — not by the device technology — so it applies to this pump exactly as it would to any single-channel dispenser.

The pump's ~5 µL/stroke target falls in the highlighted 0.5–20 µL band, which requires a 0.001 mg (1 µg, semi-micro) balance — the compliant requirement for this device.

Where the prototype stands

The prototype's actual balance resolves to 0.1 mg — about two orders of magnitude coarser than the 0.001 mg this band requires. This is the single binding metrological constraint on the prototype's test campaign, and the workarounds used to test under it (slope method, per-stroke CV via √N, same-instrument pipette comparison) are documented with their full justification in Section 10, Actual prototype protocol & deviations.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 Table 3 (minimum requirements for balances).

04

Mass → volume conversion — ISO 23783-2 Annex A

Self-contained within Annex A — not ISO 8655-6

Every gravimetric measurement yields a mass, not a volume. Converting the weighed mass gain into a delivered volume requires a correction (the Z-factor) for the density of the test liquid at test temperature and the buoyancy of air on both the liquid and the calibration weight used to verify the balance. ISO 23783-2 Annex A is self-contained for this conversion — it defines the buoyancy formula, the air-density formula, and the water-density formula directly. An earlier working assumption in this project's planning stated the Z-factor basis was ISO 8655-6; the primary-source read corrects this: Annex A does not delegate the Z-factor calculation to ISO 8655-6 — ISO 23783-2 §6.1 Note 2 cross-references ISO 8655-6 only for the balance hardware requirements behind Table 3 (Section 3), not for this mass→volume arithmetic.

Formula A.1 — general volume-from-mass (buoyancy-corrected)

$$V_L \;=\; (m_L - m_E)\cdot\frac{1}{\rho_L}\cdot\frac{1-\dfrac{\rho_A}{\rho_B}}{1-\dfrac{\rho_A}{\rho_L}}$$
  • V_L — delivered liquid volume at test temperature
  • m_L, m_E — loaded and empty (tare) vessel mass
  • ρ_L — test-liquid density at test temperature (Formula A.3, for water)
  • ρ_A — air density (Formula A.2)
  • ρ_B — calibration-weight density — Note 2: 8.0 g/mL for the stainless-steel weights used to verify the balance

Formula A.2 — air density (CIPM-2007 equation)

$$\rho_A \;=\; \rho_A(t_A,\, p,\, h_r) \qquad \text{CIPM-2007 air-density equation}$$

Air density is computed from air temperature t_A, barometric pressure p, and relative humidity h_r via the internationally-adopted CIPM-2007 formula, valid over 15–27 °C / 600–1100 hPa / 20–80% RH — a window that comfortably covers Table D.1's 17–30 °C test-condition range (Section 2).

Formula A.3 — the Tanaka formula (pure-water density)

$$\rho_W \;=\; a_5\!\left[\,1 - \frac{(t_W+a_1)^2\,(t_W+a_2)}{a_3\,(t_W+a_4)}\right]$$
  • a1 = −3.983035 °C
  • a2 = 301.797 °C
  • a3 = 522528.9 °C²
  • a4 = 69.34881 °C
  • a5 = 0.999974950 g/mL

t_W is the water temperature in °C. This is the same Tanaka reference-density formula used throughout ISO 3696 grade-3-water gravimetric verification.

Formula A.4 — the practical shortcut

$$V_i \;=\; m_i \cdot Z$$

Once Z (in µL/mg) is looked up for the test temperature and barometric pressure, every subsequent mass reading m_i converts to a volume V_i by a single multiplication — Table A.1 tabulates Z directly so this step never requires re-deriving Formulas A.1–A.3 by hand.

05

Replicates & statistical convention

The dual frame in action: Annex D leaves count open, ISO 8655 closes it

ISO 23783-2 Annex D.5.3(g) itself does not fix a replicate count — it instructs to "perform as many measurements as required," a specification-level matter it delegates onward (the same ISO 23783-3 gap named in Section 7). The operative replicate convention actually used in this protocol comes from ISO 8655: a minimum of ten measurements (n=10) per volume, repeated at three test points — 100%, 50%, and 10% of the nominal delivered volume. This is exactly the dual-frame division of labor from Section 1 in practice: the method (how to weigh) is Annex D; the replicate convention (how many measurements, at what points) is ISO 8655.

Trueness and precision, defined

$$e_T \;=\; \frac{\bar{V} - V_0}{V_0}\times 100\%$$

Trueness (systematic error, e_T) compares the mean delivered volume ̄V over n replicates against the nominal target volume V0.

$$CV \;=\; \frac{s}{\bar{V}}\times 100\%$$

Precision is expressed as the coefficient of variation (CV) — the sample standard deviation s across the replicate set, normalised by the mean ̄V. Both figures are computed per test point (100%/50%/10% of nominal), never pooled across points.

Batch-mean vs stroke-to-stroke CV — not the same measurement

Pinning the mean delivered volume with a small replicate count is metrologically a different task from characterising single-dose CV. A batch of, say, three 200-stroke deliveries pins the mean tightly (averaging suppresses per-stroke noise) but says little about how much any one stroke varies from the next — the quantity the ISO 8655 n=10 single-dose convention is actually designed to characterise. This distinction matters twice over: it is the metrologically correct way to read a trueness/CV result, and it is exactly the split the bottom layer's actual test rigs are built around (batch-mean rig vs stroke-to-stroke-CV rig) — see Section 10.

Prerequisite: the evaporation blank

Before any of the n=10/three-point replicate measurements begin, the ≥10 blank measurement cycles required by Annex D.5.2 (Section 2) must already have characterised the vessel's evaporation rate under the actual test conditions — otherwise evaporation loss during the weighing interval would be misread as dispensing error and would contaminate both the trueness and the CV figures computed here.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 Annex D.5.3(g) (replicate count left open); ISO 8655 (n=10 / 100%-50%-10% test-point convention, 2022 revision).

06

Trueness & precision — the pass/fail benchmark

ISO 8655-2:2022 — the pipette-grade benchmark structure

The pass/fail benchmark this protocol borrows by analogy (Section 1) is ISO 8655-2:2022's permissible-error table. Its confirmed structure: two bounded error types — a systematic-error (trueness) limit and a random-error (precision, CV) limit — specified at each of the three test points (100%/50%/10% of nominal, Section 5), with both limits tightening toward larger volumes (smaller deliveries are permitted a wider relative tolerance).

The exact percentage values of ISO 8655-2:2022's permissible-error table at the pump's ~5 µL stroke volume are unverified — obtain via DTU library: the standard is paywalled and every attempt to retrieve the actual table this research session hit a paywall, an access block, or returned figures that could not be traced to a visible authoritative source. Only the confirmed structure above is stated as fact; no specific percentage is quoted here as if it were verified.

ISO 23783-2 Table 1 — method uncertainty, NOT a device spec

ISO 23783-2's own Table 1 (§5.1, overview of measurement methods) lists 1.4% systematic / 0.6% random as the typical error achievable by the single-channel gravimetric method itself at 0.5–20 µL, when correctly executed. This number describes how well the measurement method can resolve a delivered volume — it is not a device pass/fail specification, and it must never be read as one.

Reading "the gravimetric method can resolve to 1.4%/0.6%" as "the pump must be accurate to 1.4%/0.6%" would be a category error: one is a measurement-method uncertainty budget for the test itself, the other would be a claim about the device under test. This protocol keeps the two numbers in clearly separate boxes — the method-uncertainty figure above, and the device benchmark (ISO 8655-2, unverified exact numbers) in the section above — and never lets one substitute for the other.

Cites: ISO 8655-2:2022 permissible-error table (structure confirmed, exact values unverified); ISO 23783-2 Table 1 (§5.1, method overview, 1.4%/0.6% at 0.5–20 µL).

07

Reporting & measurement uncertainty

The honest gap: ISO 23783-3 is delegated, not held

ISO 23783-2 §9 (Reporting) states explicitly: "measurement results, traceability, and measurement uncertainty shall be reported in accordance with ISO 23783-3." Part 2 — the part this protocol is built on — does not itself specify how results, traceability, or measurement uncertainty are to be reported; that entire specification/reporting layer belongs to Part 3. ISO 23783-3 is not in this project's possession and its content is not verified here — this gap is named honestly rather than paraphrased, matching the same discipline applied to the unverified ISO 8655-2 numbers in Section 6.

Two approaches to measurement uncertainty (§8.2)

  • Whole-system statistical approach — estimate measurement uncertainty empirically from precision/bias studies and interlaboratory comparisons of the complete measurement system, without decomposing it into individual error sources.
  • Measurement-model (GUM) approach — build an explicit model of every input quantity (mass readings, density values, air buoyancy, balance calibration) and propagate each term's uncertainty through the model, per ISO/IEC Guide 98-3 (the "Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement," GUM).

ISO 23783-2 §D.7 additionally references EURAMET CG-19 ("Guidelines on the Calibration of Non-Automatic Weighing Instruments" family / gravimetric-procedure MSU guidance) as the specific calibration-guide route for estimating measurement uncertainty in this gravimetric procedure.

Thermal-expansion correction — not applicable here

ISO 23783-2 §7 defines an optional thermal-expansion correction, VL,tc = VL·[1−β(tT−tref)], for devices with a known, manufacturer-stated thermal-expansion coefficient β. This correction is not applicable to the prototype: there is no calibrated β for a 3D-printed pump housing, so the correction term is omitted rather than guessed.

Cites: ISO 23783-2 §9 (reporting delegated to Part 3, unavailable); §8.2 (whole-system statistical vs GUM/ISO IEC Guide 98-3 measurement-model approaches); §D.7 (EURAMET CG-19); §7 (thermal-expansion correction, not applicable).
