Thesis Tool: Modular Liquid Dispensing · Proto 2+

Tensioned Tube-Path
& Stroke Volume Model

An alternative path geometry for the rigid-backed peristaltic pump. The Rotor Geometry Solver assumes the tube lies on the 180° wall arc; a tube pinned at both ends under slight seating tension instead runs straight from roller to roller, wrapping each roller over a short arc. This tool computes the true fluid-packet length for the taut path, applies the incompressibility corrections for axial pre-strain (smaller lumen, thinner wall), predicts the net volume per stroke for an existing rotor, and inverts the geometry to give the corrected rotor radius for a target volume.

Model

Taut Path Geometry & Pre-Strain Corrections

Two independent physical effects are modelled. Path: between pinch points a taut tube is a straight external tangent plus a wrap of exactly $\alpha/2$ around each roller: the packet length has a closed form, and the roller wrap recovers most of the naive chord shortfall. Pre-strain: silicone is incompressible, so axial stretch $\lambda = 1+\varepsilon$ scales the lumen area by $1/\lambda$ and the wall by $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$: the second effect silently consumes printed interference.

Purpose & Motivation

Proto 01 exposed that the tube path is not actually controlled: the tube was held at one end only, so between rollers it floats somewhere between the wall arc (assumed by the Rotor Geometry Solver) and the taut chord. An uncontrolled path is worse than either geometry: it is a stroke-to-stroke variance source, because the packet length is free to change between strokes. The design decision for Proto 2+ is to pin the tube at both ends with a small seating strain, which makes the path deterministic, and makes this model, not the arc model, the correct description.

This tool does not replace the Occlusion & Displaced-Volume Model. The stadium cross-section (Part 1) and the contact-length compensation $L_c$, $\Delta\mathrm{Arc} = N_c L_c$ (Part 2) carry over unchanged and are consumed here. What it replaces is one specific assumption inside the Rotor Geometry Solver: the mapping from rotor radius to swept tube length, $\mathrm{arcNeeded} = 2\pi R / N$.

Packet Length on the Taut Path

Delivered volume per stroke is the fluid trapped between two adjacent pinch points, so the quantity that matters is the tube centreline length between pinches, whatever curve the tube takes. For a taut tube this curve is fully determined: a straight external tangent between the wrap circles of adjacent rollers, plus a wrap arc on each roller.

Straight segment. Adjacent rollers sit on the roller-centre circle $R_{cc}$, separated by angle $\alpha$. Their wrap circles have equal radius $\rho$, so the external tangent segment is parallel to the line joining the centres and has the same length, the chord of the roller-centre circle:

$$L_{\text{straight}} = 2\,R_{cc}\,\sin(\alpha/2)$$

Wrap arcs. At each pinch point the tube is locally tangent to the wall, i.e. perpendicular to the radius through that pinch. The tangent directions at two adjacent pinches therefore differ by exactly $\alpha$, and the taut tube must absorb that direction change by wrapping the rollers: by symmetry, $\alpha/2$ on each side of every straight segment. Per packet the total wrap is $\alpha$ at radius $\rho$:

$$\boxed{\,L_{\text{packet}} \;=\; 2\,R_{cc}\sin(\alpha/2) \;+\; \rho\,\alpha\,}$$

The wrap term is why the naive intuition, "a chord is $\sin(\alpha/2)/(\alpha/2) \approx 90\%$ of the arc, so expect $-10\%$", overstates the effect. At the Proto 01 design point the wrap recovers most of the shortfall: the taut packet is only $\approx 3\%$ shorter than the wall-hugging arc, and $\approx 1\%$ shorter than what the Rotor Solver assumed (see the worked comparison below).

Pre-Strain Corrections: Incompressibility

Pinning the tube at both ends requires a small axial seating strain $\varepsilon$ so it cannot go slack or wobble. Silicone is volumetrically incompressible ($\nu \approx 0.5$): stretching by $\lambda = 1+\varepsilon$ shrinks every transverse dimension by $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$ and the cross-sectional area by $1/\lambda$:

$$d_\varepsilon = \frac{d}{\sqrt{\lambda}}, \qquad w_\varepsilon = \frac{w}{\sqrt{\lambda}}, \qquad A_\varepsilon = \frac{\pi d^2}{4\lambda}$$

Two consequences. First, the delivered volume scales down by $1/\lambda$ directly: at $\varepsilon = 3\%$ that is a $-2.9\%$ volume effect, already larger than the path correction. Second, and less obvious: the printed gap $G = 2w - \delta$ is prescribed from the unstretched wall, so stretch silently eats the interference:

$$\delta_\varepsilon \;=\; 2w_\varepsilon - G \;=\; \frac{2w}{\sqrt{\lambda}} - 2w + \delta$$

At $w = 0.85$, $\delta = 0.20$: a 3% strain reduces the effective interference to 0.175 mm (still safe); occlusion would only be lost entirely at $\varepsilon \approx 28\%$. Seating tension is therefore safe for occlusion, but the tool checks $\delta_\varepsilon$ and warns as it approaches the caution band, because the margin shrinks fast if $\delta$ was set low or the wall is thinner than assumed.

Volume per Stroke & Inverse Solve

Per stroke (one roller spacing of rotation) one packet is delivered. The occlusion compensation is inherited unchanged from the displaced-volume model: $\Delta\mathrm{Arc}_{\text{total}} = N_c L_c$ with $L_c$ evaluated at the effective interference $\delta_\varepsilon$:

$$\boxed{\,V_{\text{stroke}} \;=\; A_\varepsilon \left( L_{\text{packet}} - N_c\,L_c \right)\,}$$

Because $L_{\text{packet}}$ is linear in $R$, the design question inverts in closed form. For a target volume $V_t$, the corrected rotor radius is:

$$R^{*} \;=\; \frac{V_t / A_\varepsilon \;+\; N_c L_c \;+\; 2 R_r \sin(\alpha/2) \;-\; \rho\,\alpha}{2\,\sin(\alpha/2)}$$

This replaces the Rotor Solver's $R = N \cdot \mathrm{arcNeeded} / (2\pi)$ for a tensioned build. Feasibility checks (roller collision, hub clearance, tube length) still live in the Rotor Solver: verify $R^{*}$ there after solving here.

Two readings. First, the path correction alone is small: it cannot explain Proto 01's −32% under-dispense, which the Proto 01 error budget already attributes to the missing occlusion and the shim over-squeeze. Second, the corrections compound in the same direction, and the design consequence is not small: the rotor radius for a true 5 µL stroke moves from 19.72 mm (solver) to 20.13 mm (taut, $\varepsilon=0$) to 20.37 mm (taut, $\varepsilon = 3\%$), a 0.65 mm change in a printed part where 0.1 mm is the print resolution.

Assumptions & Limits of Validity

  1. Tube pinned at both ends, taut. The path is the minimum-length curve over the rollers. If the tube is held at one end only (Proto 01/02 as built), the geometry is indeterminate between this model and the wall arc: the two bound the truth, and the difference is a variance term, not a bias.
  2. Uniform incompressible stretch. Strain is assumed uniform along the pumping section and the wall volume conserved ($\nu = 0.5$). Local strain concentration at the pinch points is absorbed by $k$.
  3. Taut-string path, no bending stiffness. The tube is treated as perfectly flexible at this scale: wrap radius $\rho = R_r + \mathrm{OD}_\varepsilon/2$, straight segments exactly straight. Bending stiffness would bulge the straights slightly outward, toward the arc, so the taut path is a lower bound on packet length.
  4. Occlusion mechanics inherited. $L_c = k \cdot 2\sqrt{2 R_r \delta_\varepsilon}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{Arc} = N_c L_c$ are taken from the displaced-volume model unchanged, with the same $k$ calibration path (Proto gravimetry / ink-press).
  5. Quasi-static, no backpressure, no re-recovery dynamics. Same scope boundary as the sibling models. Note that with a taut path the tube leaves the wall between rollers, so occlusion near the arc entry/exit deserves a check on hardware: under backpressure, moments with a single engaged pinch matter more than they do in free delivery.
