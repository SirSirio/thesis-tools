“A concept built once is also not a concept tested.”

  • Depth reaches the precision a concept can, but commits to a shape early; breadth compares hardware, but a quick build may compare two builds rather than two mechanisms.
  • The pump had both: a syringe benchmarked, a linear pump built, four rotary builds.
  • The alignment module set the size of the machine and only one of about fifty concepts was built — a carousel or a gantry might have been smaller.
  • The model gave a starting point and was never validated; the ±0.10 mm printer now makes a real sweep of rotors worth running.

  • In decentralized settings operators want an actionable result, not liquid handling for its own sake.

  • A code on the tube tells both instruments what to do: the operator loads and scans rather than configures.
  • First, a sharper use case — livestock screening and water testing pull the specification apart.

Returning, finally, to the traveler in the arrivals hall: today, their swab must still travel to a central laboratory. This thesis shows that sample preparation does not have to.

A portable instrument can dispense liquids precisely and unattended. Before it can stand at an airport gate, it must be rebuilt from matured modules, tested on real reagents with real users, and paired with a reader that delivers the final diagnostic result.

Whether that traveler can one day be tested and cleared before leaving the terminal remains an open question, but it is no longer one that only a central laboratory can answer.
