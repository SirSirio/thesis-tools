A test that takes a day comes too late.

0 4 h 8 h 12 h 16 h
the swab travels to a central laboratory 26 h ›››
a portable molecular test at the point of care 2.6 h
an answer at the gate under 1 h

The test can travel to the gate. The sample preparation cannot yet.

  • A passenger lands in a crowded arrivals hall in the first weeks of an outbreak, with a fever.
  • The officer takes a nasal swab, but confirming the virus means sending that tube to a central laboratory.
  • Hold them a day or two and it is an operational nightmare; wave them through and an unconfirmed infection leaves the terminal.
  • An answer in under an hour settles it: 26 hours through a laboratory, 2.6 hours at the point of care.

The sample is still prepared by hand: a dozen steps, six liquids, from 5 µL to 1 mL.

  • This is a field laboratory in Ethiopia: the pipettes are the whole automation.
  • Six liquids, 5 µL to 1 mL, a two-hundred-fold span inside one protocol.
  • Trained hands scatter 28 times more at the bottom of that span than at the top.
  • So the error to remove is not skill; it is the mechanism.
