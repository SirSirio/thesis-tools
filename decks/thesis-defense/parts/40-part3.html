One clock drives six pumps, because a dose is a number of steps.

smart drivers, out of stock
  1. on paper The best driver was out of stock and dearer, so the design adapted.
  2. as built One pulse train for all six heads, one enable line each.
  3. two surprises A weak adapter stalled two pumps; a motor casing radiated into the bus.
8 pins instead of twelve
  • The analytically best answer was unbuildable: smart drivers out of stock, about thirteen euro dearer.
  • Every pump meters a fixed volume per step at one speed, so one pulse train serves all six, gated by one enable line each: eight pins instead of twelve.
  • Two surprises: a half-amp adapter stalled two pumps, and a floating motor casing radiated into the bus until one 22 nF capacitor was bonded to it.
  • Breadboards here; production is a board at 24 V with charge sensing, and no firmware change.

Each pump and its vial share one printed carrier, and the carriers hold each other.

  • One carrier per channel: the pump drops in from above, the vial slides in from the front, and the suction line stays as short as it can be.
  • Two variants, identical inside: the central one screws into the rear wall, the left one adds a tall flange for the exterior side wall.
  • Dovetails plus horizontal screws, because an operator does not know which part is load-bearing.
  • Two of six channels built; the rest is capacity, not hardware.

  • The nozzle holder needed no new part: it docks on the electronics bay, two screws down and a third horizontally into the bay wall.
  • 450 mm of tubing leaves each pump head and crosses the toothed drive rack to its needle hub, unguided, hand-disconnectable.
  • The screen sits at 45 degrees because the instrument is meant to be looked down at, on a table or the bed of a truck.
  • Split on a sliding dovetail: adding the stylus clips cost one half-print. About ten prints in all.

Calibrated on itself, the machine lands within 3.4 % of the target.

  • One thousand microlitres, commanded through the whole assembled path, weighed on an analytical balance.
  • With the constant from the isolated pump bench it fell about eighteen per cent short; recalibrated on the instrument itself, minus 3.4 and plus 0.6 per cent.
  • Scatter 0.2 to 1.0 per cent, against 0.27 for a hand pipette on the same balance.
  • The constant moved between sessions, so a field instrument has to recalibrate itself.

First rack and last rack, alike by eye

  • A dual-reagent protocol, 100 and 75 microlitres, across forty tubes in five racks, start to finish without any user help.
  • What made it possible was slowing the linear axes so the steppers could push a loaded five-rack magazine without stalling.
  • Every droplet landed inside the 5 mm target radius; three of forty wetted the deck or the lane instead.
  • First and last rack held comparable volumes by eye. On the bench supply, not on battery.

The failures were a tube cap, a tired battery and a leaking septum.

Two racks, then the draining pack could not hold both pumps at once.

the septum

It reseals about five times, and not reliably past twenty.

  • A cap left flat projects sideways and rubs the lane wall; the axis is open-loop, so the firmware thinks the rack arrived while it lagged behind.
  • On the 12 volt pack the machine ran two racks, sixteen samples, then both pumps stalled together. The firmware cannot yet run one pump at a time.
  • The vial septum leaks after about five punctures and does not reliably reseal past twenty: that is where the spillage risk lives.
  • None of the three is kinematic. All three have known fixes.

  • Performance: accuracy, reproducibility and versatility met; feasibility was a concept gate, not a test of the built device.
  • Automation: a complete forty-tube run finished without intervention. Training was deferred, so it stands as not tested.
  • Portability partly met, spillage and operator exposure partly met; cross-contamination is prevented by architecture, not by washing.
  • Four requirements are excluded honestly: they wait on an enclosure and a real circuit board, not on the mechanisms.
