The test can travel to the gate. The sample preparation cannot yet.

  • A passenger lands in a crowded arrivals hall in the first weeks of an outbreak, with a fever.
  • The officer takes a nasal swab, but confirming the virus means sending that tube to a central laboratory.
  • Hold them a day or two and it is an operational nightmare; wave them through and an unconfirmed infection leaves the terminal.
  • An answer in under an hour settles it: 26 hours through a laboratory, 2.6 hours at the point of care.

The sample is still prepared by hand: a dozen steps, six liquids, from 5 µL to 1 mL.

Designed and built by Marius Schiller.
  • The vial is inverted and sealed by a septum that recloses after the needle leaves.
  • Copper tape on the cartridge is the capacitive electrode; spring pins in the sleeve carry the signal out. Two-point calibration, within 5 %.

One clock drives six pumps, because a dose is a number of steps.

smart drivers, out of stock
  1. on paper The best driver was out of stock and dearer, so the design adapted.
  2. as built One pulse train for all six heads, one enable line each.
  3. two surprises A weak adapter stalled two pumps; a motor casing radiated into the bus.
8 pins instead of twelve
  • The analytically best answer was unbuildable: smart drivers out of stock, about thirteen euro dearer.
  • Every pump meters a fixed volume per step at one speed, so one pulse train serves all six, gated by one enable line each: eight pins instead of twelve.
  • Two surprises: a half-amp adapter stalled two pumps, and a floating motor casing radiated into the bus until one 22 nF capacitor was bonded to it.
  • Breadboards here; production is a board at 24 V with charge sensing, and no firmware change.

Each pump and its vial share one printed carrier, and the carriers hold each other.

  • One carrier per channel: the pump drops in from above, the vial slides in from the front, and the suction line stays as short as it can be.
  • Two variants, identical inside: the central one screws into the rear wall, the left one adds a tall flange for the exterior side wall.
  • Dovetails plus horizontal screws, because an operator does not know which part is load-bearing.
  • Two of six channels built; the rest is capacity, not hardware.

“A concept built once is also not a concept tested.”

  • Depth reaches the precision a concept can, but commits to a shape early; breadth compares hardware, but a quick build may compare two builds rather than two mechanisms.
  • The pump had both: a syringe benchmarked, a linear pump built, four rotary builds.

Returning, finally, to the traveler in the arrivals hall: today, their swab must still travel to a central laboratory. This thesis shows that sample preparation does not have to.

A portable instrument can dispense liquids precisely and unattended. Before it can stand at an airport gate, it must be rebuilt from matured modules, tested on real reagents with real users, and paired with a reader that delivers the final diagnostic result.

Whether that traveler can one day be tested and cleared before leaving the terminal remains an open question, but it is no longer one that only a central laboratory can answer.
